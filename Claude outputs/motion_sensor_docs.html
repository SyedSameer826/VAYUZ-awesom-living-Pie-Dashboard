How the Room Occupancy System Works

A plain-language guide to how three sensors detect if someone is inside a room, and how alerts escalate when they stay too long.

The Three Sensors

The system uses three Zigbee sensors working together. No single sensor can tell the full story — it's the combination of all three that gives us a reliable picture.

🚪
Door Sensor
Detects when the door opens and closes. Tells us someone passed through.
👁️
Curtain Sensor
PIR sensor outside the door. Detects someone approaching or leaving the room.
📡
Motion Sensor (Master)
PIR sensor inside the room. Confirms someone is actually moving around inside.

How Entry Detection Works

The system doesn't mark the room as "occupied" from a single sensor. It waits until all three sensors confirm someone entered. The signals can come in any order — what matters is that all three happen within a time window.

1
Curtain sensor fires — someone walked up to the door from outside
2
Door opens and closes — someone passed through the doorway
3
Master motion fires inside the room — confirms a person is now inside

Once all three signals are received, the room is marked OCCUPIED and the family gets a "Room Occupied" notification. If only one or two sensors fire (say the door opens but nobody goes in), the system stays in "Entry Pending" and eventually resets — no false alarm.

Why three sensors? A door sensor alone can't tell if someone walked in or just opened the door. The curtain sensor alone can't tell if they entered the room or walked past it. All three together eliminate false positives.

How Exit Detection Works

When someone leaves, the system looks for another combination of signals:

1
Door opens and closes — someone passed through the doorway again
2
Curtain sensor fires — someone walked away from the door outside
3
Master motion goes silent — no more movement detected inside

Once all three conditions are met, the room flips to VACANT and the family gets a "Room Vacant" notification.

The Room States

At any moment, the room is in one of four states:

Vacant
→
Entry Pending
→
Occupied
→
Exit Pending
→
Vacant
Vacant Nobody is in the room. Waiting for entry signals.
Entry Pending Some sensors fired but not all three yet. Waiting for the rest.
Occupied Confirmed — someone is inside. Timer starts for alerts.
Exit Pending Exit signals started. Waiting for confirmation they actually left.

What Happens While Someone Is Inside

Once the room is occupied, two types of monitoring kick in:

1. Stillness Alert

If the master motion sensor stops detecting movement for more than 1 minute and no other sensor activity is seen, the system sends an emergency notification: "No movement detected — please check." This catches situations where someone might have collapsed or become unresponsive.

2. Long Stay Alerts (Escalating)

If someone stays in the room beyond normal durations, alerts escalate in three levels:

Level Time Severity What Happens
Level 1 30 min warning Simple notification — "Someone has been in the room for over 30 minutes."
Level 2 35 min critical Stronger notification — "Extended stay — please check in."
Level 3 40 min emergency Full-screen P0 alert on the app. Requires acknowledgement from the family member.
Repeat Every 5 min after L3 emergency Keeps repeating with updated time until the room becomes vacant or family acknowledges.
Night mode: The thresholds can be configured differently for nighttime hours (e.g., bathroom visits at 2 AM may have longer acceptable durations).

Safety Ceiling

If the room stays occupied for 4 hours regardless of any other alerts, a safety ceiling alert fires. This is the absolute backstop — something is clearly wrong if someone has been in a room for that long without leaving.

Time Windows

The system uses time windows to avoid false triggers:

Entry window 2 min
Exit window 5 min
Exit silence timeout 1 min
Stillness threshold 1 min
Long stay L1 / L2 / L3 30 / 35 / 40 min
Emergency repeat interval 5 min
Safety ceiling 4 hours

These are production values. Test mode uses shorter timers (3 / 3.5 / 4 min for long stay, 30-second repeat) for quick walk-through testing.

How It All Connects

Here's the data flow from sensor to notification:

Sensor triggers → Zigbee radio signal → ZBDongle-E (USB coordinator on Pi) → Zigbee2MQTT (translates to MQTT message) → MQTT broker on the Pi → Backend service (room_occupancy_service.js processes the state machine) → Cron job every 30 seconds (occupancy_alert_checker.js checks all occupied rooms for alert thresholds) → Push notification to the family's phone app + Socket event for real-time dashboard update.

Key Things to Know

✓
The system is passive — the elderly parent doesn't need to do anything. No buttons to press, no app to use. The sensors work silently.
✓
All three sensors must agree before the room is marked occupied. This prevents false alarms from pets, curtain movement, or doors opening in the wind.
✓
PIR motion sensors detect movement only — they cannot see through walls, identify who the person is, or record any video/audio.
✓
Alert levels are configurable per room and can have different settings for day vs night.
!
PIR sensors can't detect a person sitting perfectly still. That's what the stillness alert is for — if motion stops, it flags it.
